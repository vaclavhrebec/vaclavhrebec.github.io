How to show a note

Choose Left or Right under Hand, and hold that hand up with the palm towards the camera. Touch a joint of a finger with the tip of your thumb: the base of each finger, its two middle joints, its tip.

The three notes on the thumb itself (Γ ut, A re, B mi) you show the other way round: touch the thumb with the tip of your curled index finger.

Γ ut and e la mi both bring the index tip and the thumb tip together. For Γ ut keep the thumb straight and press the index fingertip down onto the tip of the thumb. For e la mi bend the thumb and press it up into the pad of the index fingertip. When the hand leaves room for doubt, the app follows the melody and takes the smaller leap: after d la sol re it hears e, after A re it hears Γ ut. For C fa ut keep the index finger straight and touch its base.

ee la sits on the back of the middle finger: curl it and touch its nail with the thumb.

Two hands

Turn the phone sideways, choose Both, and show both palms. Each hand is a voice of its own. The right hand carries the same gamut as a mirror image: the left-hand spiral turns anticlockwise, the right-hand spiral clockwise.

If the app mixes up two places, press Teach my hand and show each position once, or Tricky places to practise the look-alikes. When a single note is read wrongly while you play, turn on Correct a note, keep your hand where it is and tap the right place on the painted hand. The app then knows your hand instead of an average one and remembers it on this device.

Musica ficta and the text

With Musica ficta: By height, the camera picture is cut into three bands. Raise your palm into the top band and C, F and G sound as C♯, F♯ and G♯; lower it into the bottom band and E, B and A sound as E♭, B♭ and A♭. D never changes: the twelve keys of a meantone keyboard allow one alteration per note.

Under Sing a text, choose a sentence of the Mass: the voice, sung by Chimera, takes the next syllable with each new note of each hand. Press Follow my singing and sing the sentence yourself: the app listens and keeps both voices on the word you have reached, finishing a word and moving on to yours as soon as it can – three voices on one text.
