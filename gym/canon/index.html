    Starting note

    A comes at the second, third, sixth or seventh copies your melody step by step along the scale, as in the Renaissance: if you sing E, a canon at the upper second answers F; if you sing F, it answers G. Such voices are played by the organ. If you tell the app your starting note, it follows the pitch you really sing, even a little flat or sharp.

    Latency

    ms

    Every device needs a little time between the sound leaving it and your voice coming back in. Raise this until the comes enters exactly with the metronome, or press Clap test and clap with the eight clicks.

    Patterns to practise

    Short duxes for two voices that make a correct canon with the comes: only consonances against it, no parallel fifths or octaves. Every one was checked note by note. Sing a pattern, then continue it, then leave it and improvise. This is how canons were learnt in the 16th century, from tables of such formulas.

    Real canons to study

    Open the Library of canons: every canon in the masses, motets and songs of Josquin, Ockeghem, La Rue, Busnoys, Compère and their contemporaries, sorted by the interval of the answer, each saying which voice follows which. Practise sets this page to that interval and entry; See the score opens the piece in Cantare super librum, where you can hide the following voice and improvise it yourself.

    How it works
    1. Choose the number of voices, then the canon: each card shows when every voice enters (left to right) and how high it sings (up and down); your voice, the dux, is red.
    2. Choose after which note value the comes enters, and the tempo of the minim. The hand beats the tactus: down on the first minim of every semibrevis, up on the second.
    3. Put on headphones and press Start. Sing one note for every value you chose; each comes repeats what you sang, after its entry and at its interval, and everything is written on the staves as you sing.
    4. Show me the rules lists the steps your melody may take in this canon, and the steps never to take one after another. While you sing, it lights up the steps that are possible from where you are.

    The canons offered are the dispositions that leave the dux the most freedom: for each, all melodies were checked so that every voice sounds only consonances against the lowest one (a fourth only between upper voices) with no parallel fifths, octaves or unisons. The bars on each card show how much freedom it leaves. The rules count steps of the scale; take care of mi contra fa (B against F).

    Your voice shifts the recording of your singing to the new pitch. Organ follows the pitch you sing with a soft organ stop.
