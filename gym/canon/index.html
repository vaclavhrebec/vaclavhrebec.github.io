Improvising canons

Sing the dux. The comes follows.

Put on headphones, press Start and sing.
The comes answers you after the entry you chose.

Canon

    In a canon at the second, third, sixth or seventh the comes copies your melody step by step along the white keys, so the size of its intervals changes with the note: if you sing E, a canon at the upper second begins on F (a minor second above); if you sing F, it begins on G (a major second above). The app hears your note by itself. If you tell it your starting note instead, it follows the pitch you really sing, even if you are a little flat or sharp. These canons are always played by the organ.

    Every device needs a little time between the sound leaving it and your voice coming back in. Raise this until the comes enters exactly with the metronome. Or press Clap test and clap along with the eight clicks: it measures the latency for you.

    Patterns to practise

    Short duxes that make a correct canon with the comes: only consonances against it, no parallel fifths or octaves. Every one was checked note by note. Sing a pattern, then continue it, then leave it and improvise. This is how canons were learnt in the 16th century, from tables of such formulas.

    How it works
    1. Put on headphones, so that the microphone hears only you.
    2. Choose the canon and the entry, set the tempo, and press Start. Allow the microphone when the browser asks.
    3. Sing the dux with the metronome. Each comes repeats what you sang, after its entry and at its interval: you hear yourself in imitation and can improvise the canon against it.
    4. In the picture, your line is dark and each comes has its own colour, so you can see the consonances and the parallels as they happen.

    In steps of the scale (seconds, thirds, sixths, sevenths): the comes copies your melody along the white keys of the piano, as in the Renaissance, so a canon at the upper second begins on F when you sing E and on G when you sing F. The line on the screen and the letters under it show where the comes stands. Choose a starting note if you want the app to follow your own pitch, even if you sing a little low or high; otherwise it listens to your first note.

    Your voice shifts the recording of your singing to the new pitch. Organ follows the pitch you sing with a soft organ stop, which sounds cleaner in the higher voices.

    For three voices in the usual way, choose fifth below, then fourth above with an entry of one beat: the first comes enters one beat after you a fifth lower, the second two beats after you a fourth higher. For four voices, choose rising fourths and sing the lowest part: each comes enters one beat later and a fourth higher than the one before.
